BUILDER BASE V2 — DESIGN CONSTITUTION + POST-DISCOVERY CORRECTION (verbatim from user, condensed only in whitespace)

0. CORRECTION AFTER DISCOVERY
Technical/codebase audit accepted as input. Do NOT treat the old repo's content architecture as evidence for V2. Do not automatically carry forward: Propulsion Labs, old apply tracks, old chapter structure, campus structure, old partner list, old event counts, old community counts, old Web3-heavy positioning, old homepage IA. Historical data may be useful but every item must earn its place.
Builder Base V2 is NOT primarily an event website. Positioning: BUILDER BASE — THE DISTRIBUTION LAYER FOR BUILDERS.
Two primary audiences: 1) BUILDERS should feel "I need to be part of this." 2) TECHNOLOGY COMPANIES / ECOSYSTEMS should feel "If we want to reach and activate builders in India, we should talk to Builder Base."
Communicate scale through CULTURE, DISTRIBUTION, ACTIVITY, PROOF rather than a member counter. Do not use "3,500+" anywhere. Do not add together audiences from Luma, Substack, WhatsApp etc (overlap). Exact audience numbers are not the primary storytelling device. Scale FELT before quantified. Events are evidence of Builder Base, NOT Builder Base itself. Do not redesign into a conference website, hackathon website, event discovery website or event organizer portfolio.

1. BRAND POSITIONING
"The distribution layer for builders." Not primarily: a Web3 community, DAO, hackathon organizer, events company, conference, generic developer community, job board, student club, collection of city chapters.
A distribution network connecting BUILDERS × TECHNOLOGY × PRODUCTS × ECOSYSTEMS × OPPORTUNITIES × CITIES × REAL-WORLD ACTIVATION.
Builder thinks "I keep seeing these people everywhere. I need to be part of this." Company thinks "If we want to reach builders in India, these are the people to talk to." Everyone else: "Why does Builder Base keep showing up everywhere?"
Emotion: FOMO, MOMENTUM, CREDIBILITY, CULTURE, DISTRIBUTION, BELONGING. Should feel larger than any individual event.

2. CORE BRAND IDEA
THE DISTRIBUTION LAYER FOR BUILDERS. Do not dilute with "India's largest...", "Web3 + AI community...", "developer community...", "student community...", "hackathon community...". Works across AI, Web3, developer tools, infrastructure, consumer tech, open source, startups, new developer ecosystems. Common denominator is the BUILDER.

3. LONG-TERM ASSOCIATION
"We want to grow in India." -> "Talk to Builder Base." Strategic direction, not literal copy everywhere. Credibility must feel earned not claimed. Possible later partner moment: "WE WANT TO GROW IN INDIA." Good. We know some builders. BUILD WITH BUILDER BASE →  (creative direction, not final copy).

4. SCALE WITHOUT VANITY
Don't sum channel audiences. Don't make homepage a dashboard of vanity metrics. Don't lead with follower counts. Communicate scale through evidence: packed rooms, registrations generated, builders activated, products tried, projects shipped, cities activated, repeat collaborations, recognizable ecosystems, real photographs, real builders, real output, recurring activity. "thousands of builders" / "across India" only where truthful. Visitors should ask "How big is Builder Base actually?"

5. VISUAL RESEARCH (six reference images remain primary)
40% PREMIUM RESTRAINT / RAMP-ONRAMP: whitespace, strong typography, confidence through simplicity, extremely clear hierarchy, limited UI chrome, deliberate composition, tech-company polish. Take the discipline, not necessarily Ramp's palette.
30% IMMERSIVE EDITORIAL PHOTOGRAPHY / FARCE: photography as dominant visual object, large photographic canvases, layered typography, selective floating information, cinematic treatment, immersive composition, framed photography where appropriate. Take composition. Do NOT copy surreal/AI imagery.
20% EVENT CULTURE / ITCONF: enormous typography, crowd photography, horizontal visual movement, people-first layouts, cultural energy, "I should have been there" FOMO. Take the energy. NOT a conference template.
10% RAW BUILDER INTERNET: monospace metadata, coordinates, timestamps, event/city identifiers, technical labels, bracketed links, subtle grid treatments, slightly experimental details. Sparingly; texture not definition.

6. STRIPE SESSIONS — interaction inspiration: one expanded item, neighbours as narrow slices, consistent heights, elegant focus transitions, photography integrated with information, editorial not card-dashboard. Apply to builders, cities, stories, projects. NOT a "Meet our speakers" section.

7. HACKETH / CYBER — only small details: structured metadata, coordinates, date/location blocks, mono labels, subtle grids, bracketed navigation, oversized faint section labels. NOT: blockchain cubes, neon crypto, cyberpunk, acid green, pixel-heavy type, dark Web3 protocol aesthetics, hacker clichés.

8. VISUAL PHILOSOPHY — REAL PEOPLE ARE THE BRAND. Prefer real Builder Base event photography, builders working, packed rooms, candid moments, laptops, demos, teams shipping, conversations, speakers surrounded by builders, recognizable locations, imperfect human moments, actual project screenshots. Over: abstract 3D, generated people, crypto graphics, generic illustrations, stock, meaningless gradients, fake dashboards. Between TECH COMPANY × EDITORIAL MAGAZINE × CULTURAL MOVEMENT × BUILDER NETWORK. Not a traditional community site. Not an event organizer site.

9. FOMO PRINCIPLE — from evidence, not "WE ARE HUGE". Packed room, another city, another ecosystem, builders shipping, projects, another activation -> "These people are everywhere." Possible language: YOU HAD TO BE THERE. / ROOMS FILL. BUILDERS SHIP. INDIA MOVES. Imagery does most of the work.

10. AVOID: purple/blue AI gradients, glassmorphism everywhere, glowing orbs, blockchain cubes, crypto aesthetics, cyberpunk, excessive neon, generic bento grids, generic SaaS cards, endless rounded rectangles, gradient text, excessive shadows, animated blobs, tokens/coins, fake terminal windows, AI illustrations, giant vanity counters, testimonial carousels of generic quotes. Don't make everything dark. Contrast between photographic/dark and clean editorial light sections encouraged.

11. COLOUR — Do NOT finalize accent. Builder Base appears to have a legitimate blue identity; don't abandon brand equity. Explore: A. Evolved Builder Base Blue. B. Monochrome + Builder Base Blue (warm/neutral paper and ink; blue selectively for actions, identifiers, emphasis). C. One alternative accent. For each: emotional character, brand implications, accessibility, photography compatibility, whether it feels AI/Web3/fintech, distinctiveness, light and dark surfaces. Do not implement.

12. TYPOGRAPHY — one of the strongest elements. Oversized headlines, tight display tracking, strong grotesk, editorial scale changes, sentence case where appropriate, selective intentional uppercase, small mono metadata. Headlines can occupy substantial viewport. Not Anton uppercase everywhere. Whitespace intentional. Body genuinely readable. Do not copy 9-13px body type.

13. PHOTOGRAPHY IS PROOF: "This happened." "These people were there." "Builders showed up." "Things got built." ATMOSPHERE: cinematic grade, subtle bottom vignette, rich contrast, real crowd energy. PORTRAITS: possibly greyscale at rest, selective brand-colour when active, editorial crop, minimal UI. Avoid overprocessing every image into the same artificial aesthetic.

14. HERO — cinematic, culturally significant, NOT an event landing page. Real Builder Base photo or video dominant. Primary statement: THE DISTRIBUTION / LAYER FOR BUILDERS. Minimal supporting copy. CTAs: JOIN THE BASE / PARTNER WITH US. Simple nav, e.g. Builder Base | Events Projects Stories About | For Partners ↗. Cities may be included if IA justifies. Do NOT make "next event" the hero concept. Do NOT make RSVP the primary identity. Subtle upcoming-event indicator can exist elsewhere.

15. HERO COMPOSITION — Farce + Ramp: large real photo canvas, restrained nav, significant negative space, headline integrated into or adjacent to photo, minimal metadata, sophisticated transition into next section. Tiny metadata e.g. DELHI / BHOPAL / INDORE / INDIA, or BUILDING ACROSS INDIA, or coordinates when meaningful. Do not fabricate activity or locations.

16. FOMO / CULTURE SECTION — one of the most important. Headline e.g. YOU HAD TO BE THERE. Real photos from different activations. Metadata e.g. DELHI / TERMINAL3, BHOPAL / CLAUDE, DELHI / METAMASK, BHOPAL / MLH, INDORE / ... ONLY factually verified combos. Interaction: horizontal photo movement, edge-bleeding images, editorial captions, subtle motion, changing city/event metadata. Purpose: "I missed this." then "I don't want to miss the next one." Not explaining every event.

17. ECOSYSTEM / COMPANY PROOF — demonstrate DISTRIBUTION CAPABILITY. No generic "TRUSTED BY 50+ COMPANIES" logo cloud. Accurate relationship terms: partner, sponsor, co-host, ecosystem collaborator, community partner, venue partner, supported by, event ecosystem, activation. Don't imply partnerships that didn't exist. Quiet, editorial, confident.

18. STORIES / ACTIVATIONS — real stories not feature cards. e.g. CLAUDE × BHOPAL, TERMINAL3 × DELHI, METAMASK × DELHI (subject to verification). Large photography, very little copy, location, context, meaningful outcome, project/building evidence, link to deeper story.

19. BUILDERS — "NOT FOLLOWERS. BUILDERS." Not decorative testimonials; they ARE the network. Show person, project, city, what they built, what they contributed to. Stripe-like one focused + neighbours partially visible. Not a corporate testimonials component.

20. PROJECTS — what came out of programs/activations: project, builder/team, event/context, city, product imagery, real link. "PEOPLE DON'T JUST ATTEND. THEY BUILD HERE." Outcomes not attendance.

21. CITIES / DISTRIBUTION — India can be an important visual component. Do NOT carry old chapter architecture. City appears only with meaningful verified activity. CITY -> ACTIVATIONS -> PHOTOS -> BUILDERS -> PROJECTS. Visually communicate DISTRIBUTION; not a generic chapter directory.

22. WHAT BUILDER BASE DOES — extremely simple. e.g. DISCOVER (products, ecosystems, opportunities, people worth knowing), BUILD (hackathons, build days, workshops, experiments, products), DISTRIBUTE (great products meet the builders who should use them). Not final copy.

23. PARTNER CONVERSION — near bottom, dramatic change of pace. "WE WANT TO GROW IN INDIA." Good. We know some builders. BUILD WITH BUILDER BASE →. Confident, memorable, not a lead-gen form. Visitor should already understand WHY.
